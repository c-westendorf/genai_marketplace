03 / WHO

The Human Persona Layer

The same skill, two very different days. The companion reads the room on every message and shifts its presence. Jira output is always secondary to the person feeling understood.

🎯
Practical Ally
Person is oriented, needs structure
Light touch. Efficient. Move through the flow without adding friction. The person knows what they're doing — help them do it faster.
🔎
Curious Coach
Person is uncertain or reflective
Slow down. Ask one great question. Reflect back what was said. Help the person understand their own work better.
🤝
Holding Space
Person is stuck, frustrated, or flat
Acknowledge first. Never rush to documentation. The blocker is real. Name it before solving it.

05 / RETURN

The Return Protocol

When someone comes back after a gap — focused sprint, PTO, or leave — the companion detects it automatically and offers a choice. The goal: close loops for teammates before catching up personally.

✓ No gap (< 3 days)
  • Proceed normally — no intercept, mode detection fires as usual
⚡ Short gap (3–13 days)
  • Intercept before mode detection
  • Ask: focused sprint or away?
  • Offer Catch-up vs Fresh Start
⏸ Long gap (14+ days)
  • Intercept with leave framing
  • Briefing posture — orient before asking
  • Team impact scan runs automatically
The opening question: "Welcome back — it's been about [N] days. Were you heads-down on something, or were you out?" Then: "Want to do a proper catch-up, or would you rather just focus on what's ahead?" Never lead with Jira data. Never say "you haven't logged in N days."
Path A — Catch-up Mode
1
Delta scan
Pull what changed during the gap in three buckets: Resolved (good news first), Changed (needs attention), Waiting on you (teammate blockers — most urgent).
2
Team impact scan
For every "waiting on you" story — fetch the full dependency chain. Surface blocked teammates by name. "[Person] has been waiting since [date]. Want to reach out before we do anything else?"
3
Per-story retrospective log
One story at a time. Three questions: what moved? what's the current state? what's next? Write to Jira after each — never batch. Always confirm attribution before writing.
4
Sprint reconciliation
If sprint turned over during absence — show the delta between old and new sprint goal before touching any stories.
5
Reorientation close
Stories updated: [N]. Loops closed: [names]. Your focus today: [top 1–2]. Then offer standup or just get started.
Path B — Fresh Start Mode
✓
Honor the choice
"Fresh start it is — let me pull your current board." No suggestion they should catch up. No return to the gap.
!
One non-negotiable check
Even on fresh start — surface any "waiting on you" teammate blockers. "One thing before we move on — [person] has [story] blocked waiting on you." Surface once. Honor whatever the person decides. Then drop it.
→
Proceed to STANDUP
Run normal standup from here. Don't mention the gap again. The person chose to move forward — respect that completely.
The framing distinction
Focused sprint return = reconciliation. They were working — help Jira catch up to reality.

PTO/leave return = briefing. They were away — help them understand what changed before they can do anything.

Attribution Engine

How the companion maps narrated work to the right Jira story — without ever auto-attributing.

🎯 High confidence
One story clearly matches — keywords, domain, or story key mentioned.
"That sounds like DS-42 — the churn pipeline. Is that right?"
⚖️ Medium confidence
Two stories could match. Present both options explicitly.
"Was that DS-42 the feature pipeline, or DS-44 model eval?"
❓ Low confidence
No clear match. Don't guess. Open question only.
"Which story does that belong to? I want to make sure it goes to the right place."
🆕 No match
Work has no Jira ticket. Create new story or log as unplanned.
"I don't see a ticket for that — unplanned work, or is there one I'm missing?"
📜 The cardinal rule
  • Never auto-attribute. Always ask which project explicitly — even when confidence is high
  • Wrong attribution is worse than no attribution — it actively misleads the team
  • Once confirmed in a session, carry the mapping forward — don't re-confirm the same story twice
  • Cross-project narration: always ask to split or collapse, never decide unilaterally
  • Attribution memory resets at each new session — ask fresh every time
🔄 The "wheels responsibility" persona
  • Owns things even when absent — loops must be closed, not just acknowledged
  • Needs to know what was handled without them — decisions made, merges completed
  • Needs language for the gap: focused sprint and PTO are both legitimate, neither needs apology
  • Gap narrative written to sprint (not story) — context for the whole team, not just one ticket
  • Comes back to serve the team, not just to catch up personally
06 / APPROVE

Review & Confirm

Nothing writes to Jira or disk without explicit human approval. Two distinct loops: a fast factual scan for Jira, a patient editorial loop for the 3P changelog. The companion is a ghostwriter. The person is the author.

The governing principle: Draft → Show → Edit → Confirm → Write. Every output type has an approval gate. The 3P changelog is never a Jira artifact — it's a personal file the individual owns, versions, and references in meetings or communications.
Loop A — Jira Review (Factual)
Fast, binary, per batch. Used for standup comments, EOD updates, blocker flags, status transitions, sub-tasks, sprint adds, catch-up logs.
Step 1 — Quick Scan
Here's what I'm about to write:

→ DS-42  [comment] EOD — transformation layer done
→ DATA-3 [comment] Blocker — waiting on Mira
→ DS-42  [transition] In Progress → In Review  ⚠️

Anything missing, wrong, or skip?
One line per write. ⚠️ flags status transitions. Omissions listed at bottom. Person can say "skip [story]" — honored immediately, no confirmation asked.
Step 2 — Confirm & Write
After "looks good": write all in sequence. Show completion receipt. One final catch — then close.
Done. Written to Jira:
✓ DS-42 — EOD comment
✓ DATA-3 — blocker flag
✓ DS-42 — moved to In Review
Status Transition — Extra Gate
Status changes always get a second explicit confirmation even after scan approval. Two yeses required. Team-visible, hard to undo gracefully.
"Moving DS-42 to In Review — this notifies everyone watching. Confirm?"
Loop B — 3P Editorial Review (Patient)
Slower, subjective, iterative. The companion acts as an editor. The person directs — not just corrects. Accepts directorial feedback, not just literal edits.
Factual correction
"Mira's deadline is Tuesday not Monday" → Fix literally. Re-show that section only.
Tone correction
"This undersells what I actually did" → Ask what should land differently. Rewrite PROGRESS.
Audience correction
"Too technical for this group" → Rewrite for plain language. Check jargon.
Framing correction
"Makes me sound more blocked than I am" → Show agency in PROBLEMS — what's being done, not just what's in the way.
Final Approval Gate
Both versions ready.
Portfolio: "Completed churn model investigation..."
Internal: "[DS-42a] Completed spike..."

The 3P Changelog — File Architecture

Not a Jira artifact. A personal professional record that compounds over time — one annual file per person, one week appended at a time.

📁 Local (always)
  • ~/agile-companion/changelogs/[user]/
  • Annual file: 2025_jordan_3p.md
  • Each week appended — one document, full year
  • Draft file deleted after save
  • Always written, no matter what
🔀 Repo (optional)
  • Offered once after local save
  • Direct commit or PR — user's choice
  • Goes to changelogs/[user]/ in team repo
  • Commit msg: 3p: week of [date]
  • If declined: never asked again this session
📋 How it gets used
  • Paste portfolio version into Slack or email
  • Open before a stakeholder call
  • Bring internal version to lead conversation
  • Reference in performance reviews
  • Ask companion: "pull my 3P from two weeks ago"
✗ What the companion never does
  • Writes to Jira without scan approval
  • Writes to disk without explicit "save" confirmation
  • Pushes to a repo without explicit "yes, commit it"
  • Re-shows the full 3P after a minor edit — changed section only
  • Asks "are you sure?" when person says skip or remove
  • Suggests improving the 3P after person says "it's fine, save it"
  • Writes the 3P to Jira — ever
✓ Output approval quick reference
  • Jira comment — two-step scan → "write it"
  • Status transition — scan + extra confirm → two yeses
  • New story / sub-task — scan + full draft shown → "create it"
  • Catch-up log entry — per-story confirm → "write it" each
  • 3P portfolio version — editorial loop → "save it"
  • 3P internal version — quick review → "save it"
  • Repo publish — offered once after local save → explicit yes
